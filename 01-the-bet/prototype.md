# The Prototype Bet

## What I Built
<!-- One sentence: what does this prototype demonstrate? -->
A single-screen dispatch console for one 32-bed medical-surgical unit that takes an incoming physical-work request, shows the qualification decision the system made - which performer, why, what prior adjudications support it, and what proof will close the job - and lets a charge nurse override it in one click, capturing the override as labeled signal.

## AI Value Archetype
<!-- Automator / Copilot / Oracle / Creator / Orchestrator -->

**Orchestrator** (dominant) with an **Oracle** secondary.

Orchestrator sets the economics and the governance burden: this chains tools and performers, the infrastructure spend is real, and autonomy risk is the governing constraint. The Oracle layer is the qualification judgment and the demand prediction - surfacing that a payload class and an embodiment are mismatched, and that a request is about to arrive, are both hidden signal no fleet vendor can produce. Copilot describes the UI and not the value.

## The Bet in One Sentence
<!-- What you're building, for whom, why now -->

For charge nurses and unit supervisors on inpatient medical-surgical units, we are building the layer that decides which qualified performer - any vendor's robot or a human runner - takes each physical-work request and what evidence closes it, now, because hospitals are buying their second and third fleet before anyone owns the arbitration between them, and because the operational record that makes the decision safe is one we already hold and a robot vendor structurally cannot.

## Kill Criteria
<!-- When would you stop? What evidence would kill this bet? -->

Stop the bet if, after 8 weeks live on one med-surg unit in our hospital, any of these hold:

1. **Adoption.** Fewer than 55% of eligible off-unit errands enter the queue. Nurses walking anyway means the queue is not where the work lives.
2. **Proof.** Verified-completion rate stays below 90%. Without the closed loop we are a request form, and Epic Systems already has one.
3. **Economics.** Median nurse-minutes returned per shift under 12. Below that, annual value per unit falls under ~$70k and will not carry integration cost.
4. **Compounding.** Fewer than 200 new labeled overrides captured, or zero rules promoted through the eval gate. A corpus that does not grow and does not promote is a stock, and the Data Advantage score was wrong.

Premise failures are separate and harder: if Premise2 returns a join rate below 60%, stop regardless of the four criteria above, because the moat argument was built on something that does not exist.

Any single trigger pauses expansion. Two triggers kill the bet.
